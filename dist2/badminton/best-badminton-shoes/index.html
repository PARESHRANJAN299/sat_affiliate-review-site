Return terms differ between the Grey and Navy pages, so check the exact colour page. Weight, upper material, box contents and the sizing basis (UK or India) are not listed. We could not confirm that the sole is non-marking. Stock by size showed as still loading, so check that too.

How to choose

Look for a non-marking sole. Many indoor courts do not allow shoes that leave marks, and dark rubber soles can do that. None of the three shoes here had 'non-marking' stated for that exact model on the pages we read. Ask the seller before you buy.

Match the shoe to how often you play. If you play now and then, a basic shoe is usually enough. If you play often, you may want more cushioning and support.

Do not use running shoes. Badminton shoes are made for side steps and quick stops and starts. Running shoes are built for moving forward and usually have a taller, less steady sole.

Check the size carefully. Sizing changes by brand. A UK shop page for another Yonex shoe says Yonex shoes tend to fit small. Read the size chart, check the return terms, or try the shoe in a store.

Weight is a trade-off. Lighter shoes suit fast footwork. Heavier ones often add cushioning and durability. Indian listings often leave out weight. For the Aerus Z, Yonex shows 240 g and an Indian retailer shows 250 g.

Read warranty, returns and stock on the exact listing. The same model can have different return terms on different colour pages, as with the BS 190. Stock also changes fast: the Yonex pages we opened in India were sold out. Confirm sizes and availability first.

Common questions

Can I play badminton in running shoes?

It is better not to. Running shoes are made for forward movement and usually have a taller, less steady sole. Badminton shoes are made for side steps and quick stops.

What is a non-marking sole and why does it matter?

It is a sole that does not leave dark marks on the floor. Many indoor courts expect it. None of the three shoes here had it confirmed on the pages we read, so ask the seller.

Should a beginner buy a Yonex shoe?

Not necessarily. Someone just starting out can begin with a basic shoe like the Kuikma BS 190. A more cushioned or lighter shoe makes more sense once you play often.

Are these shoes hand-tested?

No. This guide uses shop pages and maker statements. We mark what is the maker's claim and what we could not confirm.
