How to choose

Start smooth, then go firmer. Decathlon says smooth foam gives a soft, not very intense massage, and hard foam is stronger. So if you are new, start smooth. Move up only if it feels too mild.

Think about length. A longer roller covers more of your body at once. A shorter one is easier to pack. Two of the three pages here do not show the size, so check the size on the shop page.

Do you need vibration?. A vibrating roller adds a battery and charging. That means more weight and a limited run time. One US reviewer of the Vyper Go calls the battery life short.

Check the material and size on the live page. Material, size and weight can differ between listings for the same roller. Two of the three picks here do not show them. Read the current shop page first.

Look at warranty and returns. Check the terms on the exact listing you are buying from. Be careful with grey-market imports. They may have no brand warranty.

Stop if it hurts. If a roller causes sharp or lasting pain, stop and ask a qualified professional. This is general safety advice, not a claim about any product.

Common questions

Should I buy a smooth or a hard foam roller?

If you are new, a smooth roller is the usual start. Decathlon describes smooth foam as soft and hard foam as stronger. Move to a firmer one only if smooth feels too mild.

When can I use a foam roller?

Decathlon's category page says rollers can be used before, during or after exercise. That is the maker's wording, not our claim.

Is a vibrating roller worth it?

Not for everyone. Vibration adds a battery, charging and a limited run time. A simple roller does the basic job. A vibrating one mainly adds convenience.

What if rolling hurts?

Stop. If you feel sharp or lasting pain, ask a qualified professional. Do not push through it.
