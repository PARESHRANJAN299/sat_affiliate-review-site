How to choose a badminton racket

Check the model. Match the model code and generation before comparing listings. A similar product name does not establish the same specification.

Try the grip and weight. Printed weight classes describe a range, not how a racket will feel in your hand. Try a comfortable grip and controlled swing before choosing.

Read stringing instructions. Use the advice for the exact racket and weight class. Ask a qualified stringer if you are unsure, and confirm whether the seller supplies strings.

Check the complete cost. Compare the current price, any stringing cost and delivery. Verify seller identity and warranty coverage before paying.

Common questions

What is the difference between a head-heavy and a head-light racket?

A head-heavy racket has more weight at the top. How that balance feels depends on the player and the rest of the racket’s design. A head-light racket has more weight near the handle. Try the balance rather than assuming it will improve your game. An even-balance model sits between those descriptions.

Should I buy a racket that comes with strings?

For a beginner, yes. It is ready to play and usually strung at a gentle tension. Many mid-range and premium rackets come without strings so you can choose your own. If you buy one of those, set aside money for stringing, and ask the shop to follow the maximum tension printed on your racket.

When should a beginner buy a better racket?

When your game changes, not on a fixed date. For example, when you play often and feel the racket is holding back your power or control, or when the frame is damaged. Ask a coach or stringer whether a change of grip or strings would help before replacing the frame.

Is the racket the same on Amazon, Flipkart and Decathlon?

Do not assume listings contain the same generation or pack. Some sellers send it strung and some do not. Grip size and colour can also vary. Read the listing carefully, buy from an authorised seller when you can, and check the markings on the racket when it arrives.
