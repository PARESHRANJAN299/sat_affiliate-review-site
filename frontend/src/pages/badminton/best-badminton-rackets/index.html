Our three picks

Best for most players

Yonex Arcsaber 11 Play

Verdict: a light, easy racket for most people. A good choice if you play a few times a week.

The Arcsaber 11 Play is Yonex's entry racket in its Arcsaber line, which is made for control. It is light, so it is easy to swing and move, which helps at the net and in doubles. Yonex says its head shape makes the "sweet spot" about 7% bigger. The sweet spot is the part of the strings that hits the shuttle best, so a bigger one forgives small mistakes. In India it is usually sold with strings already fitted, so you can play right away.

Two things to know. It only takes strings up to about 27 lbs of tension, which may not suit people who like very tight strings. And shops do not agree on small details like grip size (G4 or G5). Check the print on the racket when it arrives.

Best for strong players

Yonex Astrox 99 Pro

Verdict: a powerful racket for hard smashes. Only worth it if you already play well, especially singles or from the back of the court.

The Astrox 99 Pro is a top Yonex power racket. It is a little longer than normal, heavy at the top, and very stiff. Together these help you hit deep clears and fast smashes. Shop reviews rate its smash and clear very highly. Yonex also says a part at the base of the shaft softens vibration, which is said to help touch shots at the net. The downside is that flat drives and defence take more effort, and net play is slower than with Yonex's Astrox 88S Pro.

It is not a beginner racket. The stiff shaft can tire you out if your technique or fitness is not there yet. Fake copies have been reported on some marketplace listings, so buy from an authorised Yonex seller. Stock changes by listing, and some Amazon.in pages showed as unavailable when we checked.

How to choose a badminton racket

Weight. Rackets are marked 3U, 4U and so on. 4U is roughly 80–85 g and 3U is roughly 85–90 g. A lighter 4U racket is quicker and easier on the wrist, so most players pick it. Heavier rackets add power but tire you faster.

Balance. This is where the weight sits. A racket that is heavy at the top gives more power for clears and smashes. One that is light at the top is quicker for defence and net shots. A balanced racket is in between and works for most players.

Stiffness. A bendy shaft helps if your swing is slow. A stiff shaft suits fast swings and gives sharper control, but it feels hard and tiring if your technique is not ready.

String tension. Tighter strings give more control but need better timing and put more strain on the frame. Every racket has a maximum tension printed on it. Never go above it. Beginners should stay at the lower end.

Grip size. Grips come in sizes like G4 and G5, and G5 is the smaller one. If you are unsure, pick the size you can hold without squeezing hard. You can add an overgrip to make it thicker.

Budget. Spend more only as your game improves. A mid-range racket from a known brand is usually the best value if you play a few times a week.

Common questions

What is the difference between a head-heavy and a head-light racket?

A head-heavy racket has more weight at the top. It gives more power on clears and smashes but is slower in defence and at the net. A head-light racket has more weight near the handle. It swings faster and suits quick doubles play. A balanced racket sits in between.

Should I buy a racket that comes with strings?

For a beginner, yes. It is ready to play and usually strung at a gentle tension. Many mid-range and premium rackets come without strings so you can choose your own. If you buy one of those, set aside money for stringing, and ask the shop to follow the maximum tension printed on your racket.

When should a beginner buy a better racket?

When your game changes, not on a fixed date. For example, when you play often and feel the racket is holding back your power or control, or when the frame is damaged. Many players move from a starter racket to a mid-range one in their first year. Changing the strings every few months helps more than buying a new racket.

Is the racket the same on Amazon, Flipkart and Decathlon?

The model is the same, but listings can differ. Some sellers send it strung and some do not. Grip size and colour can also vary. Read the listing carefully, buy from an authorised seller when you can, and check the markings on the racket when it arrives.
