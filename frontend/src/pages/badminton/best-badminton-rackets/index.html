How to choose the right racket

Weight. Rackets are graded by a "U" number: 4U is roughly 80–84 g and 3U roughly 85–89 g. A lighter 4U racket is quicker to swing and easier on the wrist, which is why it is the common choice for most players. Heavier rackets give more momentum but tire you sooner.

Balance. Where the weight sits changes how the racket behaves. Head-heavy rackets add power to clears and smashes. Head-light rackets are faster in defence and at the net. Even balance sits in between and suits most all-round players.

Shaft flex. A flexible shaft helps players with slower swings generate power. A stiff shaft rewards fast, well-timed swings and gives precise control, but it feels harsh and tiring if your technique is not ready for it.

String tension. Higher tension gives more control and a crisper feel but needs better timing and puts more stress on the frame. Every racket has a maximum tension printed on the frame; do not exceed it. Beginners are usually better off at the lower end of the range.

Grip size. Rackets come in grip sizes such as G4 and G5; G5 is the smaller one. If you are unsure, pick the size that lets you hold the racket without gripping hard, then add an overgrip to fine-tune.

Budget. Spend more on the racket only as your playing improves. A mid-range racket from a reputable brand is usually the best value for someone playing a few times a week.

Frequently asked questions

What's the difference between head-heavy and head-light rackets?

A head-heavy racket carries more of its weight toward the top of the frame, which adds power to clears and smashes but makes fast defence and net play slower. A head-light racket carries weight toward the handle, so it swings and recovers faster and suits quick doubles exchanges. Even-balance rackets sit between the two.

Do I need to buy a racket that comes pre-strung?

No, but it is the easier route for beginners. A pre-strung racket is ready to play out of the box, usually at a modest tension. Many mid-range and premium rackets are sold unstrung so you can choose your own string and tension; in that case budget for stringing, and have it done by a racket shop that knows the maximum tension printed on your frame.

How often should a beginner upgrade their racket?

There is no fixed schedule. Upgrade when your game changes, not on a calendar: for example, when you play regularly and feel the racket limiting your power or control, or when the frame is damaged. Many players move from a starter racket to a mid-range one within their first year of regular play. Restringing every few months matters more than buying a new racket.

Are these rackets the same on Amazon, Flipkart and Decathlon?

The model is the same, but listings differ: some sellers ship pre-strung rackets while others ship unstrung, and grip size or colour may vary. Always check the listing details, buy from the brand's authorised seller where possible, and check the frame markings when your racket arrives.
